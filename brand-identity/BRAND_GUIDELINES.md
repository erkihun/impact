# Brand Identity Guidelines

**Addis Ababa City Administration — Technical and Vocational Education and Training Bureau (TVET Bureau)**
Version 1.0 · Identity proposal

> Institutional titles are used exactly as supplied and are never translated, abbreviated or re-typed:
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | Amharic | የአዲስ አበባ ከተማ አስተዳደር · የቴክኒክና ሙያ ትምህርትና ሥልጠና ቢሮ |
> | English | ADDIS ABABA CITY ADMINISTRATION · TECHNICAL AND VOCATIONAL EDUCATION AND TRAINING BUREAU |

**Brand idea.** Knowledge → Skills → Innovation → Productivity → Sustainable Development.

All four concepts are drawn on one 240 × 240 grid, as pure vector paths, with one construction rule: **no two shapes overlap**. Separation is always a real gap and holes are real holes, so every concept reproduces in one colour, in reverse, and on transparent backgrounds without loss.

## 1. Meaning of each logo concept

| # | Concept | Silhouette | Idea in one line |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Integrated Skills & Technology** | Gear (circle) | A learner at the centre of the machine: technical education holding a person whose raised arms are an open book. |
| 02 | **Knowledge to Productivity** | Rising wedge | One open book. Its left page is Knowledge; the right page becomes three saw-tooth roof steps that climb to a figure on the summit. |
| 03 | **Green TVET & Innovation** | Hexagon | Engineering precision (nut / cell) sheltering a seedling whose veins are circuit traces; the leaves also read as raised arms. |
| 04 | **Institutional Excellence** | Heraldic shield | Authority and trust: three rising chevrons (graded competency) beneath a star of excellence. |

## 2. Symbolism of the geometric elements

Colour roles are constant across concepts: **blue = structure**, **green = knowledge / growth**, **orange = spark of innovation (one per mark)**.

**01 · Skills & Technology**
- *Ten-tooth gear* (blue) — technical education and industry; even teeth = standardised, systematic training.
- *Circular aperture* (negative space) — frames the learner like an official seal.
- *Open book* (green) — knowledge and competence; the two pages double as the learner's raised arms.
- *Orange sphere* — the learner's head and the spark of innovation.

**02 · Knowledge to Productivity**
- *Left page* (green) — Knowledge, the start of every pathway.
- *Saw-tooth steps* (blue) — Skills, Innovation, Productivity. The saw-tooth is the universal profile of a workshop roof. Each step rises 40 units and drops 14, a net climb of 26: progress is cumulative.
- *Orange figure* — Sustainable Development, achieved through people; a sun over the roofline and a person at the summit.

**03 · Green TVET & Innovation**
- *Hexagon* (blue) — nut, bolt head and honeycomb cell; six equal sides, one tolerance.
- *Twin leaves* (green) — growth and sustainability; each leaf is a lens built from two equal circular arcs.
- *Vein slits* — leaf veins that double as circuit traces.
- *Stem* — a short torso, completing the double reading of "seedling" and "person with raised arms".
- *Orange node* — innovation spark; a sun, a circuit node, a head.

**04 · Institutional Excellence**
- *Shield with inset line* (blue) — trust and quality assurance; the inset line echoes a certificate border.
- *Three chevrons* — graded competency levels, rising and certified. The middle (green) chevron carries the sustainable thread.
- *Four-point star* (orange) — excellence and innovation.

## 3. Colour and its institutional relevance

| Colour | HEX | RGB | Role | Meaning | Contrast on white |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Institutional Blue | `#123B75` | 18, 59, 117 | Primary (≈ 60 %) | Trust, stability, public authority | 11.0 : 1 |
| Innovation Green | `#16A067` | 22, 160, 103 | Secondary (≈ 25 %) | Growth, renewal, Green TVET | 3.4 : 1 |
| Innovation Orange | `#E89B24` | 232, 155, 36 | Accent (≈ 5 %) | Energy, craft, ingenuity | 2.3 : 1 |
| Technical Gray | `#4F6578` | 79, 101, 120 | Neutral (≈ 10 %) | Precision, supporting voice | 6.1 : 1 |
| White | `#FFFFFF` | 255, 255, 255 | Ground | Clarity, openness | — |

Accessibility notes:
- Green (3.4 : 1) meets the 3 : 1 threshold for graphical objects but **not** for body text. Never set text in green.
- Orange (2.3 : 1 on white) is a **decorative accent only** — never text, never a thin line on white. In the logos it is a filled dot or star that carries no information the rest of the mark does not; the monochrome versions remove it entirely.
- Technical Gray is used for secondary lines of text only at ≥ 11 px equivalent (the lock-up uses it for the city-administration line).

Reversed variants use the same palette; Technical Gray text becomes `#C9D6E3` on blue so it stays readable (7.4 : 1).

In the SVG/PNG logo files the text is **converted to outlines** (it is not live text), so the lock-ups render identically everywhere without the fonts installed. For documents and web pages, set the same strings in live text using the fonts above. Fonts are bundled under the SIL Open Font License 1.1 (`assets/fonts/`).

## 5. Clear space

**X = ¼ of the emblem height.** Keep X free of text, imagery, rules and page edges on every side of the lock-up, measured from the outermost artwork (not the SVG canvas, whose built-in margin is ≈ 8 units). The page's *Guidelines → Clear space* diagram shows this for the selected concept.

## 6. Minimum sizes

| Layout | Digital | Print |
|---|---|---|
| Emblem | 24 px | 8 mm |
| Horizontal lock-up | 320 px wide | 70 mm wide |
| Vertical lock-up | 240 px wide | 45 mm wide |

Below these widths drop the lock-up and use the emblem alone, with the full title set beside it in live text. Favicons (16–32 px) use the emblem; at 16 px expect the silhouette and colour blocking, not the detail (verified on the *Scale* section of the presentation).

## 7. Backgrounds and monochrome usage

| Surface | Use |
|---|---|
| White, Mist, light neutrals | `full-color`, `mono-blue`, `mono-black`, `grayscale` |
| Institutional Blue, Navy, black, dark photography | `reversed-color` (preferred) or `reversed-white` |
| One-colour print, stamps, embossing, engraving, fax | `mono-blue` or `mono-black` |
| Black-and-white documents | `grayscale` |
| Photography | Calm area, ≥ 4.5 : 1 contrast with the mark; otherwise place on a solid panel |

Files: `assets/variants/<concept>/<layout>-<variant>.svg`, six variants per layout: `full-color`, `mono-blue`, `mono-black`, `grayscale`, `reversed-white`, `reversed-color`.

## 8. Incorrect usage

Do not: **stretch or squash** · **rotate or tilt** · **recolour outside the palette** · **add shadows, glows or 3D** · **outline or stroke** · **fade or lower opacity** · **place on busy backgrounds** · **use full colour on dark blue** · **crop or rearrange elements** · re-type or re-space the institutional titles · translate or abbreviate them.

Live examples are generated in the presentation under *Guidelines → Incorrect usage*.

## 9. Recommended primary identity

**Adopt Concept 01 — Integrated Skills & Technology — as the primary identity**, and consider **Concept 03** as a secondary mark for Green TVET programmes.

Justification (design-team judgement; the scoring matrix in the presentation shows the underlying 1–5 ratings):

1. **Reads at once.** Gear, book and person say "technical training" without explanation, to Amharic- and English-speaking audiences alike.
2. **Strongest at small sizes.** The circular silhouette survives 16–32 px, which matters for favicons, social avatars and stamps. Concept 02's detail (the saw-tooth) is the first to degrade.
3. **Built for institutional media.** A circle suits seals, certificates, ID cards, embossing and embroidery; the one-colour version loses nothing.
4. **Covers the whole philosophy** with one shape per idea: technology (gear), knowledge (book), the human at the centre, and a single spark of innovation.
5. **Honest trade-off.** The gear-and-book idea is the most conventional of the four; Concept 02 is the most distinctive but is asymmetric and harder to use in square and circular formats; Concept 04 is the most formal but says least about TVET specifically. Concept 01's distinctiveness comes from execution (ten-tooth rhythm, the figure-as-book, the single orange head) rather than from an unfamiliar idea. A trademark search is recommended before adoption of any concept.
