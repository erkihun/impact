01 — Logo concepts

Four concepts, four distinct constructions.

A circle-in-gear, a book that becomes a factory roofline, a hexagonal seedling and a heraldic shield. They share one palette, one grid (240 units) and one rule: no overlapping shapes, so every concept reproduces perfectly in a single colour.

09 — Usage guidelines

Rules that keep the identity consistent.

A summary. The complete guide, including symbolism of every geometric element, is in BRAND_GUIDELINES.md.

Clear space

Keep a margin of X = ¼ of the emblem height free of text, imagery and edges on every side, measured from the outermost artwork.

Minimum sizes

LayoutDigitalPrint
Emblem24 px8 mm
Horizontal320 px wide70 mm wide
Vertical240 px wide45 mm wide

Below these widths, drop the lock-up and use the emblem alone, with the full title set beside it in live text.

Backgrounds

  • Light surfaces (white, Mist) — full colour, Institutional Blue mono, black mono or grayscale.
  • Dark surfaces (Blue, Navy, black, dark photography) — reversed colour or reversed white only.
  • Photography — place the mark on a calm area with ≥ 4.5:1 contrast, otherwise use a solid panel.

Monochrome use

  • One-colour print, stamps, embossing, fax and engraving: mono blue or mono black.
  • Black-and-white documents: grayscale.
  • Every shape is separated by a gap, so no detail is lost in one colour.

File formats

  • SVG — print, signage and any scalable use. Opens in Illustrator and Inkscape.
  • PNG, transparent — screens and office software; 1024, 2048 or 4096 px.
  • Never re-create the lock-ups in a text editor; text is already outlined.

Incorrect usage

Recommendation

Adopt Concept 01 as the primary identity.

Use Concept 03 as a secondary mark for Green TVET programmes if the Bureau wants a dedicated sustainability badge.

  • Reads at once. Gear, book and person say “technical training” without explanation, in both Amharic- and English-speaking contexts.
  • Best at small sizes. The circular silhouette stays recognisable at 16–32 px, which matters for the favicon, social avatars and stamps.
  • Built for institutions. A circle suits seals, certificates, ID cards, embossing and uniform embroidery; one-colour versions lose nothing.
  • Covers the whole philosophy. Technology (gear), knowledge (book), the human at the centre, and a single spark of innovation.
